Custody stages

    Two lifecycles, not one

    The lake has a custody state that lasts for as long as the lake does. A restoration works project has its own stages and then ends. Confusing them is how a lake gets treated as finished because a contract closed.

    This is why restoration progress does not move the custody state on its own. Works can reach 100% with the perimeter still open.

    Where this lake sits against the wider position

    The reason the notification stages matter: as reported to the High Court in January 2025, preliminary notifications had been issued for 2,793 of 3,342 water bodies within HMDA limits, but final notifications for only 765.

    Those figures are HMDA's and relate to HMDA limits, not to HYDRA's jurisdiction, and the position will have moved since. They are cited only to make the point that the gap between stage 2 and stage 3 is where most water bodies sit, and enforcement is weak until a lake crosses it.
